= Exercises
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

The exercises on the installed `sakila` database: a section for each table and view, with a section for each level, and, at the end, a section for the schema as a whole.
Each exercise poses a problem; its answer, and what the installed data gives back, are in the `.sql` file, and are checked by an integration test.

Run a file as a whole:

[source,sh]
----
docker exec -i mysql-sakila mysql -uroot -p'Passw0rd!' -t sakila < src/test/sql-exercises/<section>/<NN>_<level>.sql
----

[[SAKILA_PAYMENT_B_01]]
==== SAKILA_PAYMENT_B_01: how much each staff member took

Group the payments by the staff member who took them.
Show each
`staff_id` with the number of payments and their sum, ordered by `staff_id`.

.`payment/01_basic.sql`, SAKILA_PAYMENT_B_01
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT s.staff_id, s.first_name, s.last_name, s.address_id, s.email, s.store_id, s.active, s.username, s.password, s.last_update, COUNT(*) AS payments, SUM(p.amount) AS total -- every column but picture, a BLOB which does not print
FROM payment p
         JOIN staff s ON s.staff_id = p.staff_id
GROUP BY s.staff_id
ORDER BY s.staff_id
----

[[SAKILA_PAYMENT_I_03]]
==== SAKILA_PAYMENT_I_03: how many payments were less than, equal to, or more than the film's rental rate

Compare each payment's `amount` with the `rental_rate` of the film it paid for, reached through the rental and the copy.
Name the result `less`, `the rate` or `more` with a `CASE` expression, and count the payments of each, ordered by that name.
A payment over the rate carries a late fee.

.`payment/02_intermediate.sql`, SAKILA_PAYMENT_I_03
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT CASE
           WHEN p.amount < f.rental_rate THEN 'less'
           WHEN p.amount = f.rental_rate THEN 'the rate'
           ELSE 'more' END AS paid,
       COUNT(*) AS payments
FROM payment p
         JOIN rental r ON r.rental_id = p.rental_id
         JOIN inventory i ON i.inventory_id = r.inventory_id
         JOIN film f ON f.film_id = i.film_id
GROUP BY paid
ORDER BY paid
----

====

Answered by `Payment_Exercises_02_Intermediate_PersistenceIT`.

[[SAKILA_PAYMENT_A_02]]
==== SAKILA_PAYMENT_A_02: the runs of consecutive days on which payments were made

The payments were not made on every day; they come in runs of consecutive days.
Find each run: its first day, its last day and the number of days in it, ordered by the first day.
Number the distinct days in order; a day minus its number is the same for every day of a run, and the query groups by it (gaps and islands).

.`payment/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_PAYMENT_A_02
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
WITH days AS (SELECT DISTINCT DATE(payment_date) AS day FROM payment),
     islands AS (SELECT day, DATE_SUB(day, INTERVAL ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY day) DAY) AS island FROM days)
SELECT MIN(day) AS first_day, MAX(day) AS last_day, COUNT(*) AS days
FROM islands
GROUP BY island
ORDER BY first_day
----

====

[[SAKILA_RENTAL_I_03]]
==== SAKILA_RENTAL_I_03: the rentals overdue at the end of the data

The last rental of the data was made on 2006-02-14, so take 2006-02-15 as today.
Find the rentals not returned yet which have been out longer than the film's `rental_duration`, counting the days with `DATEDIFF`.
Show the whole rows of the rentals, the film's `title` and `rental_duration`, and the days each is overdue, the most first; on a tie, by `rental_id`.

.`rental/02_intermediate.sql`, SAKILA_RENTAL_I_03
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT r.*, f.title, f.rental_duration, DATEDIFF('2006-02-15', r.rental_date) - f.rental_duration AS days_overdue
FROM rental r
         JOIN inventory i ON i.inventory_id = r.inventory_id
         JOIN film f ON f.film_id = i.film_id
WHERE r.return_date IS NULL
  AND DATEDIFF('2006-02-15', r.rental_date) > f.rental_duration
ORDER BY days_overdue DESC, r.rental_id
----

[[SAKILA_RENTAL_A_01]]
==== SAKILA_RENTAL_A_01: how many rentals came back later than the film's rental duration allows

Each film has a `rental_duration`, the number of days a rental may last.
Count the rentals which were returned later than that: the `return_date` is after the `rental_date` plus the film's
`rental_duration` days.
The duration is on the film, so the query goes from the rental through the copy to the film, and does arithmetic on dates.

.`rental/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_RENTAL_A_01
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT COUNT(*) AS late
FROM rental r
         JOIN inventory i ON i.inventory_id = r.inventory_id
         JOIN film f ON f.film_id = i.film_id
WHERE r.return_date > r.rental_date + INTERVAL f.rental_duration DAY
----

====


[[SAKILA_RENTAL_A_02]]
==== SAKILA_RENTAL_A_02: the five longest waits between a customer's two rentals in 2005

For each rental made in 2005, find the same customer's rental just before it, and the number of days between the two.
Show the five longest waits: the `customer_id`, the earlier and the later `rental_date`, and the days, the longest first; on a tie, by `customer_id`, then by the later `rental_date`.
The rental just before is `LAG(...) OVER (PARTITION BY customer_id ORDER BY rental_date, rental_id)`.

.`rental/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_RENTAL_A_02
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT customer_id, previous_rental, rental_date, DATEDIFF(rental_date, previous_rental) AS days
FROM (SELECT customer_id, rental_date,
             LAG(rental_date) OVER (PARTITION BY customer_id ORDER BY rental_date, rental_id) AS previous_rental
      FROM rental
      WHERE rental_date < '2006-01-01') g
WHERE previous_rental IS NOT NULL
ORDER BY days DESC, customer_id, rental_date
LIMIT 5
----

====


[[SAKILA_RENTAL_A_03]]
==== SAKILA_RENTAL_A_03: whether a copy was ever rented out twice at the same time

A copy should not be rented again before it comes back.
Join the rentals with themselves on `inventory_id`, and count the pairs where the second starts at or after the first and before the first is returned; a rental not returned yet lasts to the end of time.
Each pair is counted once; the answer is `0`.

.`rental/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_RENTAL_A_03
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT COUNT(*) AS overlapping
FROM rental a
         JOIN rental b ON b.inventory_id = a.inventory_id AND b.rental_id <> a.rental_id
WHERE b.rental_date >= a.rental_date
  AND b.rental_date < COALESCE(a.return_date, '9999-12-31')
  AND (b.rental_date > a.rental_date OR b.rental_id > a.rental_id)
----

====

Answered by `Rental_Exercises_03_Advanced_PersistenceIT`.

UNION ALL SELECT 'city', COUNT(*) FROM city
UNION ALL SELECT 'country', COUNT(*) FROM country
UNION ALL SELECT 'customer', COUNT(*) FROM customer
UNION ALL SELECT 'film', COUNT(*) FROM film
UNION ALL SELECT 'film_actor', COUNT(*) FROM film_actor
UNION ALL SELECT 'film_category', COUNT(*) FROM film_category
UNION ALL SELECT 'film_text', COUNT(*) FROM film_text
UNION ALL SELECT 'inventory', COUNT(*) FROM inventory
UNION ALL SELECT 'language', COUNT(*) FROM language
UNION ALL SELECT 'payment', COUNT(*) FROM payment
UNION ALL SELECT 'rental', COUNT(*) FROM rental
UNION ALL SELECT 'staff', COUNT(*) FROM staff
UNION ALL SELECT 'store', COUNT(*) FROM store
----

==== SAKILA_SCHEMA_A_01: the customer who paid the most in each of the five countries which paid the most

Sum each customer's payments, and their country's.

[source,sql]

                       JOIN address a ON a.address_id = cu.address_id

                       JOIN country co ON co.country_id = ci.country_id
              GROUP BY co.country, cu.customer_id, cu.first_name, cu.last_name),
     ranked AS (SELECT paid.*,
                       RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY country ORDER BY total DESC) AS rank_in_country,
                       SUM(total) OVER (PARTITION BY country) AS country_total

WHERE rank_in_country = 1
ORDER BY country_total DESC

[[SAKILA_SCHEMA_A_06]]
==== SAKILA_SCHEMA_A_06: how many actors are one film, and how many two films, away from KEVIN BLOOM; a Bacon number, Sakila having no KEVIN BACON

Two actors are one film apart when they appear in the same film; two films apart when they are one film apart from someone who is one film apart from the first.
Starting from `KEVIN BLOOM`, walk `film_actor` with a recursive common table expression, up to two steps, keep each actor's smallest number of steps, and count the actors at one and at two.
Sakila has no `KEVIN BACON`, so this is the Bacon number of KEVIN BLOOM.

.`__/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_SCHEMA_A_06
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
WITH RECURSIVE bacon (actor_id, number) AS (
    SELECT actor_id, 0 FROM actor WHERE first_name = 'KEVIN' AND last_name = 'BLOOM'
    UNION DISTINCT
    SELECT fa2.actor_id, b.number + 1
    FROM bacon b
             JOIN film_actor fa1 ON fa1.actor_id = b.actor_id
             JOIN film_actor fa2 ON fa2.film_id = fa1.film_id
    WHERE b.number < 2)
SELECT number, COUNT(*) AS actors
FROM (SELECT actor_id, MIN(number) AS number FROM bacon GROUP BY actor_id) t
WHERE number > 0
GROUP BY number
ORDER BY number
----

==== SAKILA_SCHEMA_A_08: the five longest runs of days on which a customer rented every day

.`__/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_SCHEMA_A_08
[%collapsible]

[source,sql]

FROM islands i
         JOIN customer c ON c.customer_id = i.customer_id
GROUP BY c.customer_id, i.island
ORDER BY days DESC, c.customer_id, first_day
