= Exercises
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

The exercises on the installed `sakila` database: a section for each table and view, with a section for each level, and, at the end, a section for the schema as a whole.
Each exercise poses a problem; its answer, and what the installed data gives back, are in the `.sql` file, and are checked by an integration test.

Run a file as a whole:

[source,sh]
----
docker exec -i mysql-sakila mysql -uroot -p'Passw0rd!' -t sakila < src/test/sql-exercises/<section>/<NN>_<level>.sql
----

[[SAKILA_ACTOR_B_01]]
==== SAKILA_ACTOR_B_01: the actors of a given last name, ordered by actor_id

Find every actor whose `last_name` is exactly a given name; here the name is `GUINESS`.
Show the whole rows, ordered by
`actor_id`.
A single table and an equality filter; no join.

.`actor/01_basic.sql`, SAKILA_ACTOR_B_01
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT *
FROM actor
WHERE last_name = 'GUINESS'
ORDER BY actor_id
----

[[SAKILA_ACTOR_I_06]]
==== SAKILA_ACTOR_I_06: the five actors whose films are, on average, the longest

For each actor, take the average `length` of the films they appear in, through `film_actor`.
Show the five actors with the largest average: their `actor_id`, `first_name`, `last_name` and the average rounded to two decimal places, the largest first.
Break ties by `actor_id`.

.`actor/02_intermediate.sql`, SAKILA_ACTOR_I_06
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----

LIMIT 5
----

[[SAKILA_ACTOR_I_07]]
==== SAKILA_ACTOR_I_07: the films of the first five actors, counted by rating

For each of the actors `1` to `5`, count their films of each rating, one column per rating.
Show the `actor_id`, the `first_name`, the `last_name` and the five counts, ordered by `actor_id`.
Each column is a `SUM` of a `CASE` that gives `1` for a film of that rating and `0` otherwise, so one pass over the rows fills all five columns.

.`actor/02_intermediate.sql`, SAKILA_ACTOR_I_07
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT a.*,
       SUM(CASE WHEN f.rating = 'G' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)     AS g,
       SUM(CASE WHEN f.rating = 'PG' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)    AS pg,
       SUM(CASE WHEN f.rating = 'PG-13' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS pg_13,
       SUM(CASE WHEN f.rating = 'R' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)     AS r,
       SUM(CASE WHEN f.rating = 'NC-17' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS nc_17
FROM actor a
         JOIN film_actor fa ON fa.actor_id = a.actor_id
         JOIN film f ON f.film_id = fa.film_id
WHERE a.actor_id BETWEEN 1 AND 5
GROUP BY a.actor_id
ORDER BY a.actor_id
----

[[SAKILA_ACTOR_I_08]]
==== SAKILA_ACTOR_I_08: the actors who never appeared in a G-rated film

A family promotion shows only actors of `G`-rated films; find the actors it cannot use.
Find the actors none of whose films is rated `G`, the whole rows, ordered by `actor_id`.
A `NOT EXISTS` whose subquery joins `film_actor` to `film`, so that the condition on the rating is inside the subquery, not on the outer row.

.`actor/02_intermediate.sql`, SAKILA_ACTOR_I_08
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT a.*
FROM actor a
WHERE NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1
                  FROM film_actor fa
                           JOIN film f ON f.film_id = fa.film_id
                  WHERE fa.actor_id = a.actor_id
                    AND f.rating = 'G')
ORDER BY a.actor_id
----

[[SAKILA_ACTOR_A_01]]
==== SAKILA_ACTOR_A_01: the actors who appear in a film of every rating

Find the actors who have appeared in at least one film of each rating (`G`, `PG`, `PG-13`, `R`, `NC-17`).
This is a relational division: for each actor, count the distinct ratings of their films, and compare that count with the number of distinct ratings over all films, taken by a subquery, so the query does not hard-code the five ratings.
Show the whole rows of the first five such actors by `actor_id`.

.`actor/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_ACTOR_A_01
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT a.*
FROM actor a
         JOIN film_actor fa ON fa.actor_id = a.actor_id
         JOIN film f ON f.film_id = fa.film_id
GROUP BY a.actor_id, a.first_name, a.last_name
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT f.rating) = (SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT rating) FROM film)
ORDER BY a.actor_id
LIMIT 5
----

====


[[SAKILA_ACTOR_A_02]]
==== SAKILA_ACTOR_A_02: the actors in the top seven film counts, ties included

Rank the actors by the number of films they appear in, so that actors with the same number share a rank and the next number takes the next rank.
Show the actors of the first seven ranks: `actor_id`, `first_name`, `last_name`, the number of films and the rank, ordered by the rank and then by `actor_id`.
Unlike the `LIMIT` of SAKILA_ACTOR_I_02, `DENSE_RANK()` keeps every actor tied at the last rank.

.`actor/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_ACTOR_A_02
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT actor_id, first_name, last_name, films, film_rank
FROM (SELECT a.actor_id,
             a.first_name,
             a.last_name,
             COUNT(*)                                   AS films,
             DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY COUNT(*) DESC) AS film_rank
      FROM actor a
               JOIN film_actor fa ON fa.actor_id = a.actor_id
      GROUP BY a.actor_id, a.first_name, a.last_name) ranked
WHERE film_rank <= 7
ORDER BY film_rank, actor_id
----

====


[[SAKILA_ACTOR_A_03]]
==== SAKILA_ACTOR_A_03: the longest film of each of the first five actors

For each of the actors `1` to `5`, find the longest film they appear in.
Show the `actor_id`, the `first_name`, the `last_name`, the film's `title` and its `length`, ordered by `actor_id`; if two films of an actor share the longest length, both are shown, ordered by `title`.
A correlated subquery takes the longest length of the actor of the outer row.

.`actor/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_ACTOR_A_03
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT a.*, f.*
FROM actor a
         JOIN film_actor fa ON fa.actor_id = a.actor_id
         JOIN film f ON f.film_id = fa.film_id
WHERE a.actor_id BETWEEN 1 AND 5
  AND f.length = (SELECT MAX(f2.length)
                  FROM film_actor fa2
                           JOIN film f2 ON f2.film_id = fa2.film_id
                  WHERE fa2.actor_id = a.actor_id)
ORDER BY a.actor_id, f.title
----

[[SAKILA_ACTOR_INFO_A_01]]
==== SAKILA_ACTOR_INFO_A_01: the actors whose film_info disagrees with the tables on the number of categories

Check the view against the tables it is built on.
For each actor, compare the number of categories in `film_info`, counted as in SAKILA_ACTOR_INFO_I_02, with the number of distinct categories of the actor's films, counted from `film_actor` and `film_category` by a correlated subquery.

The installed data has none.

.`actor_info/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_ACTOR_INFO_A_01
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT ai.*
FROM actor_info ai
WHERE CHAR_LENGTH(ai.film_info) - CHAR_LENGTH(REPLACE(ai.film_info, ':', ''))
          <> (SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT fc.category_id)
              FROM film_actor fa
                       JOIN film_category fc ON fc.film_id = fa.film_id
              WHERE fa.actor_id = ai.actor_id)
ORDER BY ai.actor_id
----

[[SAKILA_FILM_TEXT_A_01]]
==== SAKILA_FILM_TEXT_A_01: the most common kind of story in each category

Every `description` starts the same way, like `A Epic Drama of ...`: its third word is the kind of story, such as `Drama`, `Documentary` or `Saga`.
For each category, find the kind of story its films tell the most, and show the category, the kind and its number of films, ordered by the category.
Ties are broken by the kind, the first in alphabetical order.
Take the third word with `SUBSTRING_INDEX`, count the films per category and kind in one CTE, and rank them with `ROW_NUMBER()` in another.

.`film_text/03_advanced.sql`, SAKILA_FILM_TEXT_A_01
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
WITH kinds AS (SELECT c.name AS category,
                      SUBSTRING_INDEX(SUBSTRING_INDEX(ft.description, ' ', 3), ' ', -1) AS kind,
                      COUNT(*) AS films
               FROM film_text ft
                        JOIN film_category fc ON fc.film_id = ft.film_id
                        JOIN category c ON c.category_id = fc.category_id
               GROUP BY c.name, kind),
     ranked AS (SELECT category, kind, films,
                       ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY category ORDER BY films DESC, kind) AS rn
                FROM kinds)
SELECT category, kind, films
FROM ranked
WHERE rn = 1
ORDER BY category
----

FROM chain
ORDER BY step
