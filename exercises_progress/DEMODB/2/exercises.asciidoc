= Exercises
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3

The exercises on the installed `demo` database, the PostgreSQL demonstration database (https://postgrespro.com/community/demodb[Airlines]), in its `bookings` schema: a section for each table and view, with a section for each level, and, at the end, a section for the schema as a whole.
Each exercise poses a problem; its answer, and what the installed data gives back, are in the `.sql` file.
The data is the `3m` edition, three months of flights from 2025-10-01, and the database's today, `bookings.now()`, is 2025-12-01; the views read the names in the language `bookings.lang()` returns, `en`.

Run a file as a whole:

[source,sh]
----
docker exec -i postgresql-demodb psql -U postgres -d demo < src/test/sql-exercises/<section>/<NN>_<level>.sql
----

[[DEMODB_FLIGHTS_B_01]]
==== DEMODB_FLIGHTS_B_01: the flights of the route PG0001 in October 2025

Find the flights whose `route_no` is `PG0001` and whose `scheduled_departure` is in October 2025.
Show the whole rows, ordered by `scheduled_departure`.
A half-open range on a timestamp: from the first of the month, up to but not including the first of the next.

.`flights/01_basic.sql`, DEMODB_FLIGHTS_B_01
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT *
FROM flights
WHERE route_no = 'PG0001'
  AND scheduled_departure >= '2025-10-01'
  AND scheduled_departure < '2025-11-01'
ORDER BY scheduled_departure
----

==== DEMODB_FLIGHTS_B_04: the flights scheduled to take more than twelve hours

Subtract the scheduled departure from the scheduled arrival, and keep the flights whose difference is over twelve hours.
Show the flight, the route and the difference, the longest first; flights with the same difference are ordered by `flight_id`.

[[DEMODB_FLIGHTS_I_02]]
==== DEMODB_FLIGHTS_I_02: the ten routes whose flights depart the latest

For the flights that arrived, the delay is the actual departure minus the scheduled one.
Average it per route, in minutes to one decimal, and keep the ten routes with the longest average; routes with the same average are ordered by `route_no`.
Show the route, the average and how many flights it averages.
`EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM ...)` turns an interval into seconds.

.`flights/02_intermediate.sql`, DEMODB_FLIGHTS_I_02
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT route_no,
       ROUND(AVG(EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM actual_departure - scheduled_departure)) / 60, 1) AS delay_minutes,
       COUNT(*)                                                                      AS flights
FROM flights
WHERE status = 'Arrived'
GROUP BY route_no
ORDER BY delay_minutes DESC, route_no
LIMIT 10
----

[[DEMODB_FLIGHTS_I_04]]
==== DEMODB_FLIGHTS_I_04: today's flights, with their airports

The database's today is the date of `bookings.now()`.
Find the flights scheduled to depart today, and join each to the row of `routes` valid at its scheduled departure.
Show the flight, the route, the two airports, the scheduled departure and the status, ordered by the scheduled departure and then by `flight_id`.

.`flights/02_intermediate.sql`, DEMODB_FLIGHTS_I_04
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----

FROM flights f
         JOIN routes r ON r.route_no = f.route_no AND r.validity @> f.scheduled_departure
WHERE f.scheduled_departure::date = bookings.now()::date
ORDER BY f.scheduled_departure, f.flight_id
----

[[DEMODB_FLIGHTS_A_03]]
==== DEMODB_FLIGHTS_A_03: the five fullest flights of the first day

For the flights scheduled on 2025-10-01 that arrived, count the boarding passes and the seats of the flight's airplane, and keep the five with the highest share of seats boarded; flights with the same share are ordered by `flight_id`.
Show the flight, the route, the airplane, the two counts and the share as a percentage to one decimal.
Three joins, one of them on a range containment, a group, and a subquery for the seats.

.`flights/03_advanced.sql`, DEMODB_FLIGHTS_A_03
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT f.flight_id,
       f.route_no,
       r.airplane_code,
       COUNT(bp.ticket_no)                                                AS boarded,
       s.seats,
       ROUND(100.0 * COUNT(bp.ticket_no) / s.seats, 1)                    AS boarded_pct
FROM flights f
         JOIN routes r ON r.route_no = f.route_no AND r.validity @> f.scheduled_departure
         JOIN (SELECT airplane_code, COUNT(*) AS seats FROM seats GROUP BY airplane_code) s
              ON s.airplane_code = r.airplane_code
         JOIN boarding_passes bp ON bp.flight_id = f.flight_id
WHERE f.status = 'Arrived'
  AND f.scheduled_departure >= '2025-10-01'
  AND f.scheduled_departure < '2025-10-02'
GROUP BY f.flight_id, f.route_no, r.airplane_code, s.seats
ORDER BY boarded_pct DESC, f.flight_id
LIMIT 5
----

[[DEMODB_FLIGHTS_A_04]]
==== DEMODB_FLIGHTS_A_04: the time since the previous flight of the route PG0001, in October 2025

For each flight of the route `PG0001` scheduled in October 2025, show how long after the previous one it departs.
The first has no previous flight, and its gap is `NULL`.
`LAG` reads the previous row of the window; one `timestamptz` minus another is an `interval`.
The gaps of two days are the Thursdays, the day the route's `days_of_week`, `{1,2,3,5,6,7}`, leaves out.

.`flights/03_advanced.sql`, DEMODB_FLIGHTS_A_04
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT flight_id,
       scheduled_departure,
       scheduled_departure - LAG(scheduled_departure) OVER (ORDER BY scheduled_departure) AS since_previous
FROM flights
WHERE route_no = 'PG0001'
  AND scheduled_departure >= '2025-10-01'
  AND scheduled_departure < '2025-11-01'
ORDER BY scheduled_departure
----

[[DEMODB_BOOKINGS_A_01]]
==== DEMODB_BOOKINGS_A_01: how many bookings do not add up

A booking's `total_amount` should be the sum of the prices of the segments of its tickets.
Join the three tables, group by booking, and count the bookings whose total differs from the sum.
A `HAVING` comparing a column of the group with an aggregate; the count is of the groups.

.`bookings/03_advanced.sql`, DEMODB_BOOKINGS_A_01
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT COUNT(*) AS bookings
FROM (SELECT b.book_ref
      FROM bookings b
               JOIN tickets t ON t.book_ref = b.book_ref
               JOIN segments s ON s.ticket_no = t.ticket_no
      GROUP BY b.book_ref, b.total_amount
      HAVING b.total_amount <> SUM(s.price)) x
----

[[DEMODB_BOOKINGS_A_03]]
==== DEMODB_BOOKINGS_A_03: the share of the money each tenth of the bookings brings

Split the bookings into ten groups of the same size, the most expensive first, and show for each the bookings, the money and its share of all the money, in percent to one decimal.
`NTILE(10)` deals the rows into the groups; a window over an aggregate, `SUM(SUM(...)) OVER ()`, puts the grand total on every row.
Bookings of the same amount on either side of a boundary may land in either group, which changes no sum.

.`bookings/03_advanced.sql`, DEMODB_BOOKINGS_A_03
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT decile,
       COUNT(*)                                                             AS bookings,
       SUM(total_amount)                                                    AS total,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(total_amount) / SUM(SUM(total_amount)) OVER (), 1) AS share_pct
FROM (SELECT total_amount, NTILE(10) OVER (ORDER BY total_amount DESC) AS decile
      FROM bookings) b
GROUP BY decile
ORDER BY decile
----

[[DEMODB_BOARDING_PASSES_A_01]]
==== DEMODB_BOARDING_PASSES_A_01: the passengers of the first day seated in other fare conditions than they paid

For the flights scheduled on 2025-10-01, find the boarding passes whose seat's `fare_conditions`, on the flight's airplane, differs from the segment's.
Count them, and count them per direction: upgraded, a seat of better fare conditions than paid for, and downgraded.

.`boarding_passes/03_advanced.sql`, DEMODB_BOARDING_PASSES_A_01
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]
----
SELECT COUNT(*)                                                                              AS passes,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE rank.seat > rank.paid)                                            AS upgraded,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE rank.seat < rank.paid)                                            AS downgraded
FROM boarding_passes bp
         JOIN segments s ON s.ticket_no = bp.ticket_no AND s.flight_id = bp.flight_id
         JOIN flights f ON f.flight_id = bp.flight_id
         JOIN routes r ON r.route_no = f.route_no AND r.validity @> f.scheduled_departure
         JOIN seats st ON st.airplane_code = r.airplane_code AND st.seat_no = bp.seat_no
         CROSS JOIN LATERAL (SELECT ARRAY_POSITION(ARRAY ['Economy', 'Comfort', 'Business'], st.fare_conditions) AS seat,
                                    ARRAY_POSITION(ARRAY ['Economy', 'Comfort', 'Business'], s.fare_conditions)  AS paid) rank
WHERE f.scheduled_departure >= '2025-10-01'
  AND f.scheduled_departure < '2025-10-02'
  AND st.fare_conditions <> s.fare_conditions
----

[[DEMODB_SCHEMA_A_04]]
==== DEMODB_SCHEMA_A_04: the five airports where the most passengers change flights

A ticket of more than one segment changes flights between them, at the airport the later segment departs from, which is always the one the earlier segment arrived at.
For each segment but a ticket's first, the layover is its scheduled departure minus the previous segment's scheduled arrival.

.`__/03_advanced.sql`, DEMODB_SCHEMA_A_04
[%collapsible]
====
[source,sql]

       COUNT(*)                           AS connections,
       MIN(layover)                       AS shortest,
       DATE_TRUNC('minute', AVG(layover)) AS average
FROM (SELECT r.departure_airport                                     AS airport,
             f.scheduled_departure - LAG(f.scheduled_arrival) OVER w AS layover
      FROM segments s
               JOIN flights f ON f.flight_id = s.flight_id
               JOIN routes r ON r.route_no = f.route_no AND r.validity @> f.scheduled_departure
      WINDOW w AS (PARTITION BY s.ticket_no ORDER BY f.scheduled_departure)) c
WHERE layover IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY airport
ORDER BY connections DESC, airport
LIMIT 5
----
